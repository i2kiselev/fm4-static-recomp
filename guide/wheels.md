# Racing wheels

[← Back to the README](../README.md)

Any wheel Windows sees as a game controller works: direct drive, belt and gear wheels, with
their own pedals or separate ones. You set it up once in the launcher, and adjust the force
feedback there or in the game.

## Set up your wheel

1. Plug in the wheel and its pedals, and switch the wheel on.
2. Close your wheel's own control panel (for example Pit House, G HUB or the Thrustmaster
   Control Panel) if it is open. It can hold the wheel and stop the game's forces.
3. Start the launcher and click **WHEEL** (beside **CONTROLS**, under **PLAY**). The game must
   not be running.
4. Tick **Use a racing wheel**. The set-up starts on its own and asks you, one step at a time:
   - turn the wheel all the way to the left;
   - press the throttle all the way down, then the brake;
   - press the clutch, or click **SKIP** if you have none;
   - pull the handbrake, or click **SKIP** if you have none;
   - press the paddle or button you want for shifting up, then for shifting down;
   - press the button you want for pausing, then for selecting in menus, then for going back.
5. When it says **Set-up done**, click **SAVE**.
6. Check it on the **SETUP** tab: the bars move when you turn the wheel and press each pedal.
7. In **Wheel rotation**, set the same number of degrees as in your wheel's own software
   (900 if you never changed it).
8. Open **FORCE FEEDBACK** and click your wheel type: **DIRECT DRIVE**, **BELT** or **GEAR**.
   Your wheel's box or manual says which one it is.
9. Click **SAVE**, then **PLAY**.

If something does nothing in the game, close the game, open **WHEEL** again and click **SET** on
that row (on **SETUP** for the wheel and pedals, on **BUTTONS** for the buttons), or **RUN SETUP
AGAIN** to do every step again. Click **SAVE** after.

If **WHEEL** says **Update the game to set up a wheel.**, click **CHECK FOR UPDATES** at the
bottom of the launcher.

## Adjust the force feedback in the game

Open PC OPTIONS (**F6**, or **BACK + START** held together) and press **RB** (a shift paddle) until
**WHEEL** is shown. The tab is there only while **Use a racing wheel** is ticked.

- **WHEEL TYPE** sets every row below it for your kind of wheel.
- **STRENGTH**: turn it down if the wheel is too strong to hold.
- **STEERING WEIGHT**: up for a heavier wheel in corners, down for a lighter one.
- **SELF-CENTRING**: up to pull the wheel back to the middle harder.
- **DAMPING**: up to make the wheel slower and heavier to turn.
- **ROAD EFFECTS**: up to feel kerbs, bumps and crashes more.
- **ENGINE VIBRATION**: down if the wheel buzzes all the time.
- **CENTRE FEEL**: up if the wheel feels loose and empty when you drive straight.
- **SMOOTH JOLTS**: up to soften sudden kicks, like gear changes; kerbs stay sharp.
- **STEERING LOCK**: down to steer quicker, with less turning of your wheel for full lock.

Press **Y** (SAVE & APPLY) to keep your changes; they work as soon as you are back on the track.
Changing a row from **STEERING WEIGHT** to **SMOOTH JOLTS** turns the wheel type into **CUSTOM**.
Picking a wheel type again replaces those values: the game asks you to press **Y** a second
time first.

The launcher's **FORCE FEEDBACK** tab has the same settings, saved to the same place; there you
click a wheel type a second time to replace **CUSTOM** values. You can change it while the game
runs; it applies at the next start.

## Good to know

- The wheel has no force in menus and in the pause menu; the forces come back on the track.
- If the forces stop in the middle of a race, unplug the wheel and plug it in again.
- An H-shifter: on **BUTTONS**, click **SET ALL** beside **H-shifter gates** and put the shifter
  in each gear it asks for (**SKIP** a gear yours does not have).
- Shifters and separate pedal sets are set up the same way, but have not been tested by us yet.
  If yours does not work, [report it](reporting-issues.md).
